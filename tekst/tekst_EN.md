# “Amelia and Lumi” — English text (pages 3–30)

Str. 31–32: zob. ksiazka_pl_en.md / see ksiazka_pl_en.md

## Page 3

This is Amelia. She is six years old, with freckles on her nose and the biggest smile on the whole street. She loves stars, all the colors of the rainbow, and spinning round and round. She spins until the world goes swirly. Then she falls on the grass and laughs up at the sky.

## Page 4

Amelia has a friend. His name is Lumi. He is small, fluffy and round like a ball of wool, with a glowing star on his forehead. He has been with Amelia since the day she was born. Nobody else can see him: not Mom, not Dad, not Grandma. Only Amelia. (And you.)

## Page 5

Lumi whispers: *Listen!* Amelia closes her eyes and hears everything. A bee buzzing in the roses. A drop falling on a leaf. The fridge humming softly in the kitchen. *The world is playing a concert for us,* says Lumi, and his star glows bright.

## Page 6

*Now look!* says Lumi. Amelia sees what others miss: a ladybug with exactly four spots, and a button missing from Dad's coat. “Dad, you lost a button!” she calls. “How do you do that?” asks Dad, surprised. Amelia and Lumi just smile. It's their secret.

## Page 7

Amelia knows the names of forty stars. She also knows all the planets in order, without a single mistake. She lines up her toys like a rainbow: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple. Each one has its place. *That's our superpower,* says Lumi. *When we love something, we love it with our whole heart.*

## Page 8

Every evening is the same. A bath, the star song, and the soft blue blanket. Mom kisses Amelia on the forehead. “Good night, little star.” *Same is good,* whispers Lumi. *Same is safe.* And he falls asleep with Amelia. His star glows in the dark like a little night light.

## Page 9

On Saturday, it's cousin Max's birthday party. The flat is full of children, balloons and confetti. Everything is LOUD. POP! goes a balloon. BOOM! goes the music. Suddenly, Lumi's soft fur turns into sharp spikes.

## Page 10

“Come on, Amelia, it's fun!” the children shout. “Just a little longer, sweetie,” says Mom. But Amelia covers her ears with her hands. *Too much,* says Lumi, tangled in a prickly knot. *Too much, too much, too much.* *I know. I feel it too.*

## Page 11

Grandma knitted a new striped sweater. It's beautiful, but it scratches like a hundred tiny ants. Amelia wriggles and scratches, and finally pulls it off over her head. She stomps so hard that the crayons jump on the table. “Don't be silly,” says Grandma. “It's such a lovely sweater.” Nobody knows that Lumi feels every single ant.

## Page 12

Today is zoo day. Amelia has a plan: first the giraffes, then the penguins, and ice cream at the end. She thought about the plan all night. But in the morning, it rains. “We'll go another time,” says Dad. The plan is gone, the whole world wobbles, and tears roll down Amelia's cheeks.

## Page 13

Amelia hides under her blue blanket. It's quiet, dark and soft in there. Slowly, slowly, Lumi's spikes turn fluffy again. *I'm here,* says Lumi. *I'm always here.* Only Lumi understands how much the change of plans hurt.

## Page 14

Then a big day comes. Amelia starts school. She has a new backpack with stars on it, but she stops at the door. The building is huge, and it's noisy inside. *Will we be okay?* Lumi asks quietly. He becomes very, very small.

## Page 15

RING! goes the bell. Squeak, squeak go the shoes in the hallway. Bzzz go the lights on the ceiling. Everyone talks, laughs and shouts at the same time. For the other children, it's a normal morning. For Amelia, it's a storm, and Lumi's spikes stick out in every direction.

## Page 16

“Sit still, Amelia.” “Look at me when I'm talking.” “No humming, please.” Amelia tries very hard. She sits still, she looks, and she doesn't hum. *I'm so tired,* whispers Lumi. Trying this hard is more tiring than running.

## Page 17

At break time, Amelia takes some chalk and draws stars on the playground. “Who wants to play stars?” she asks. The children look at each other. “That's a weird game,” someone says. “Let's play tag!” Everyone runs off, and Amelia is left alone with the chalk in her hand.

## Page 18

Every day, Amelia's smile gets a little smaller. She spins less and less. She talks about stars less and less. And every day, Lumi's star gets a little dimmer. *Where is our superpower?* asks Lumi. Amelia doesn't know.

## Page 19

Under the stairs, there is a quiet spot where nobody comes. There is no bell and no lights, only dust and shadows. Amelia sits in the corner and hugs her knees tight. Lumi curls up next to her into a small, tight ball. Tears roll down Amelia's cheeks.

## Page 20

*Everyone is different from us,* thinks Amelia. *Everyone likes loud games, and nobody minds a scratchy sweater. Maybe something is wrong with me?* Then an even worse thought comes: *Maybe it would be easier… without you, Lumi.* Lumi doesn't answer. His star almost goes out.

## Page 21

Outside, it rains. Drip, drip, drip. Inside, it is quiet. Very, very quiet.

## Page 22

Suddenly, someone sits down on the lowest step. It's Leo from her class, the boy who always draws rockets in his notebook. He doesn't say, “Come out.” He doesn't say, “What's wrong with you?” He doesn't say anything. He just sits nearby. Quietly.

## Page 23

They sit like this for a long time. The rain taps on the window. At last, Leo asks quietly, “What do you need?” Nobody has ever asked Amelia that before. Lumi opens one eye.

## Page 24

Amelia thinks for a long time. “Quiet,” she says at last. “And a little time.” Leo nods. “I like quiet too. Break time is too loud for me.” Then he sees the star on her T-shirt. “Do you like stars?” Somewhere deep inside Amelia, something starts to glow.

## Page 25

Leo goes with Amelia to their teacher. “What could help you?” the teacher asks. Soon, the classroom changes a little. A quiet corner with a soft cushion appears. A picture plan for the day hangs on the wall, so nothing is a surprise. And one by one, Lumi's spikes fall away.

## Page 26

That evening, Amelia lies under her blue blanket. *I'm sorry, Lumi,* she thinks. *I thought it would be easier without you. That's not true. It was never you.* Lumi opens his eyes and cuddles up to her. His star begins to glow again.

## Page 27

And suddenly, Amelia understands. *You're not next to me, Lumi. You're inside me. You are me when I hear the bee in the roses. You are me when I count the ladybug's spots, and when noise hurts.* Lumi smiles. The star on Amelia's T-shirt glows, just like Lumi's.

## Page 28

The next day at break time, Amelia draws chalk stars again. This time, Leo draws next to her. “That's the Big Dipper,” Amelia explains. “And the bright one is the North Star.” Then Amelia is the first to hear the blackbird sing, and the first to spot the ladybug with four spots. Her superpowers are back.

## Page 29

Amelia's smile is back. Maybe it's a little quieter than before. But it's calm, it's real, and it's all hers. *We're ourselves again,* whispers Lumi. And his star glows brighter than ever.

## Page 30

Some friends you can see. Some friends live deep inside us and help us hear, see and feel. And a true friend doesn't say, “Don't be silly.” A true friend sits down next to you and asks, “What do you need?”
